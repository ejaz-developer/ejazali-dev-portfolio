A business outgrows spreadsheets when the spreadsheet stops being a tidy place to store data and starts being a fragile system holding your operations together. The tell is not the size of the file — it is the friction. If people are emailing versions around, re-typing the same customer into three places, or asking "which tab is current?", the spreadsheet has quietly become software you never meant to build. At that point it is costing more in errors and duplicated effort than a proper tool would.

Spreadsheets are wonderful until they are load-bearing. They were never designed for concurrent editing, permissions, or a workflow that four people depend on daily.

## The predictable breaking points

- **Version chaos.** Two people edit the same file; someone merges changes by eye; a formula breaks silently. The file is now a liability.
- **Duplicate entry.** The same customer or order gets typed into the spreadsheet, the invoice, and the email. Every copy is a chance to diverge.
- **No source of truth.** When five tabs can each hold "the number," nobody fully trusts any of them. Decisions get made on whichever version someone had open.
- **Work that lives in one person's head.** The person who built the formulas becomes the only one who can maintain them. That is a single point of failure, not a spreadsheet.

## What to build instead

The fix is not a giant system. It is a small web app around the one workflow the spreadsheet was really running. In practice that means: one place to enter data once, a clear view of the current state, and the handful of actions people actually take — create, update, status, send.

The goal is to keep the parts that worked — the layout people know, the fields they care about — and remove the fragility: no more version conflicts, no more re-typing, no more guessing which tab is real.

## Do you need custom software at all?

Before building anything, check whether a packaged tool already fits. Off-the-shelf software is usually the cheaper first move when your process matches what the tool assumes. Custom software earns its keep when your workflow is genuinely different — or when the tool you would buy just recreates the same copy-paste in a different interface.
