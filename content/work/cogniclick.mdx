## Challenge

A YouTube video lives or dies on the thumbnail and title, and most creators still
choose them by feel. The two options are to publish and hope, or to swap images
after the fact and lose the early window. There is no cheap way to compare a few
thumbnail and title candidates before the video goes out.

## Approach

CogniClick treats the choice as a small experiment. A creator uploads up to three
thumbnail variations for one video, adds the title, and picks the category and
audience it targets. The app scores the candidates against each other so the
decision is made on the results screen rather than in the creator's head.

The core idea is a single analysis record: one video, its candidate images and
title, and the score that came back. Everything else — history, totals, the
dashboard — reads from those records.

## What I built

- An analysis workspace that accepts up to three thumbnail variations.
- Title, category, and audience inputs to frame the comparison.
- A scoring pass that ranks the candidates.
- A dashboard with total analyses, average score, top category, and recent runs.

## Stack

- **Next.js** with the App Router for the dashboard and analysis flow.
- **TypeScript** for typed analysis records.
- **Prisma** for the data model.
- **PostgreSQL** for storing analyses and their results.

## Result & status

CogniClick is **live** at its demo URL. It is early: the dashboard shows a
handful of analyses, some runs have failed, and there is no public engagement
data to quote — so there is none here. What it does stand on is the working
product: upload variations, run the comparison, read the score, and keep the
history.
