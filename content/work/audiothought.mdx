## Challenge

The ideas people have most easily are the ones they speak, not the ones they
type. A voice note captures a plan, a meeting recap, or a train of thought in
seconds — and then it sits in a recordings folder, unstructured and hard to act
on. The raw audio is where the value is buried.

## Approach

AudioThought is built as a pipeline from unstructured speech to something a
person can use. A recording is transcribed, the transcript is cleaned up and
reduced to its points, and the result is presented as structured notes with
action items. Because a plan is often a sequence of steps, the same input can be
read as a flowchart rather than a wall of text.

Next.js handles the app and its server paths, Supabase provides the data and
authentication layer, and AssemblyAI does the speech-to-text work.

## What I built

- A recording and capture experience that accepts a voice note.
- A transcription step that turns audio into text.
- Structuring that reduces a transcript to notes and action items.
- A flowchart view for inputs that describe a sequence of steps.

## Stack

- **Next.js** for the application and its server routes.
- **Supabase** for data storage and authentication.
- **AssemblyAI** for speech-to-text transcription.

## Result & status

AudioThought is **in development**. The landing page and the capture experience
are built and shown below, and the speech-to-text pipeline runs. It is not
deployed publicly yet, and it is not presented as finished. The remaining work
is hardening the structuring step across messy, real recordings before launch.
