## Challenge

Small businesses in emerging markets tend to run on notebooks and memory. Sales
get recorded, customers get remembered, and invoices get chased by phone — until
the owner needs to know how a month actually went. Then the numbers are scattered
across ledgers, chat threads, and loose receipts, and nobody can say what the
cash flow really is.

The hard part is not adding features. It is giving a business one place where the
everyday record-keeping it already does is captured as it happens.

## Approach

I started from the records a business keeps every day rather than from a feature
list: sales, customers, invoices, and cash flow. If those four are reliable, the
reporting on top of them is trustworthy.

The app is a single authenticated workspace built on Next.js with Prisma over
PostgreSQL, so the schema is explicit and every screen reads from the same data.
Auth and Google sign-in come first because a business app is only useful once a
real account is holding the data.

## What I built

- Sales entry that builds a running record of what was sold.
- Customer ledgers that keep a history per buyer.
- Invoice management for what is owed and paid.
- Cash-flow tracking that shows money in and out over time.
- A dashboard that summarizes the business at a glance.

## Stack

- **Next.js** with the App Router for the application and its server paths.
- **TypeScript** for typed records end to end.
- **Prisma** for the data model and queries.
- **PostgreSQL** as the store for sales, customers, invoices, and cash flow.

## Result & status

Record Keeper is **live**, and honest about its size: it is a thin product. The
account and sign-in flow and the core record-keeping features work, and the app
is deployed and reachable at its demo URL. It is not yet a finished commercial
product — the interfaces are being strengthened and the reporting deepened.
